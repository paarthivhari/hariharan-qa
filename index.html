About

Quality is not a phase.
It's a discipline.

I'm a QA Lead with 7+ years specialising in Healthcare EMR and HCM/Payroll — two of the most regulated, zero-tolerance environments in enterprise software. I don't execute test cases in isolation; I understand the business logic deeply enough that developers come to me with functional questions, not the other way around.

At Dedalus Healthcare, I owned QA end-to-end for 15+ EMR upgrade releases over 5.8 years — mastering 13+ clinical modules and the full HL7 v2 interface stack. My most significant catch was a PID segment mismatch that was silently routing clinical lab results to the wrong patient. That's the kind of defect that never shows in a regression pass if you're not thinking like a clinician.

At 4i Apps Solutions, I crossed into a completely new domain — HCM/Payroll — and became the Absence Management SME within 5–6 months. The payroll arrear defect I caught there would have silently underpaid employees without a single UI error appearing. Both stories say the same thing: domain depth is what separates a test executor from a QA professional.

QA Philosophy

How I Think About Quality

Six principles that have guided my approach across 7+ years and two regulated domains.

01 ——
Domain knowledge is your sharpest testing tool
The PID mismatch I caught wasn't in a test script. I found it because I understood that HL7 segment order matters for patient identity. You can't script for what you don't understand.
02 ——
Silent failures are the most dangerous defects
Both my most significant catches — the payroll arrear and the PID mismatch — produced no UI error. The system behaved normally. Silent failures only surface when you test at the data layer.
03 ——
Risk drives coverage, not coverage percentages
100% test coverage of low-risk paths is less valuable than 80% coverage of the paths where failure has consequences. I plan coverage around what matters in production.
04 ——
Quality is built upstream, not caught downstream
The most expensive defect is the one that reaches production. I push quality left — into requirements, into dev conversations, into configuration review — before a single test runs.
05 ——
A QA lead is a quality partner, not a gatekeeper
My best collaborations happen when developers bring me questions early, not when they throw builds over a wall. I invest in those relationships because they produce better software.
06 ——
Evidence, not opinion, earns stakeholder trust
Traceability matrices, defect reports, and release QA sign-offs aren't paperwork. They're the audit trail that lets stakeholders make informed go/no-go decisions with confidence.
